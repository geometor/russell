People¶

Architects, Thinkers & Pioneers of the Wave-Field Cosmogony

Authors & Primary Pioneers¶

Walter Russell (1871–1963)

American polymath, sculptor, painter, architect, musician, and natural philosopher. Author of The Universal One (1926), The Secret of Light (1947), and co-author with Lao Russell of the Home Study Course in Universal Law, Natural Science and Philosophy (1950–1952). He formulated the continuous rhythmic balanced interchange of wave-fields, the cube-sphere geometry of space, and the nine-octave gyroscopic periodic table of the elements.

Lao Russell (1904–1988)

Philosopher, author, and co-founder with Walter Russell of the University of Science and Philosophy at Swannanoa Palace, Waynesboro, Virginia. Co-author of the 48-lesson Home Study Course (1950–1952) and Atomic Suicide? (1957).

Cited Geometers, Philosophers & Scholars¶

Nikola Tesla (1856–1943)

Electrical engineer and inventor who recognized Walter Russell’s wave-field mechanics and advised him to “lock up this knowledge in a vault for a thousand years until man is ready for it.”

Johannes Kepler (1571–1630)

Imperial mathematician and astronomer whose Harmonices Mundi (1619) established planetary orbital harmonies and polyhedral nesting, directly anticipating Russell’s cube-sphere wave-field and harmonic octave relations.

Dmitri Ivanovich Mendeleev (1834–1907)

Russian chemist whose periodic law of 1869 is analyzed and extended in Lesson 47 (Figs. 175–177), where Russell organizes the elements as a continuous nine-octave spiral of gyroscopic light-wave pressure rather than disjoint atomic weights.

Robert Kennedy Duncan (1868–1914)

Professor of Industrial Chemistry and author of The New Knowledge (1905), cited by Russell in Fig. 175 on the Periodic Law as “God’s alphabet of the universe.”

Sir Isaac Newton (1642–1727)

Formulator of classical gravitational theory, whose inverse-square attraction Russell reconstructs as an illusion of centripetal electric condensation balanced by centrifugal radiative expansion.
