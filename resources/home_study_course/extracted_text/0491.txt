     When these two charts were first published and widely distributed in 1926, they
showed seven full tones in the hydrogen octave instead of just the one which you see in
Figure 175. At that time the creator of the new charts gave a series of lectures to tell
science that the hydrogen octave consisted of seven full tones, and that hydrogen is
number 22 in the periodic table instead of number 1. Shortly after that three of these full
tones were verified by science, one of which led to the discovery of heavy water.
     Science was in a quandary, however, regarding this "discovery" because the
elements had already been numbered from 1 to 92, and a strange and impossible theory of
atomic structure had been evolved which made it impossible to renumber the elements to
make room for these verifications of the existence of other tones in the hydrogen octave
which these new Russell charts revealed.
     There was no way out of it but to call these full tone elements isotopes,
notwithstanding the fact that isotopes cannot occur in Nature until the depolarizing power
of radioactivity becomes stronger than the polarizing power of generoactivity.
